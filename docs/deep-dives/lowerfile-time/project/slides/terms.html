A slice, its stages, and its outcome

Slice, census, refusal

A slice is one top-level function of TScert's front end (the folder tools/), copied into a file of its own with the definitions of its file it uses, and checked alone. The census checks every slice and records each one's outcome and the time of every step. A check ends verified, or in a refusal: the place where no proof of the proposed types was found, and why.

Stages

A check runs separate programs, one after another. parse: TypeScript's compiler reads the slice and each module it imports into syntax documents. lower: Lean, a proof assistant, derives each function's control-flow graph. propose: an OCaml program, the producer, proposes a type, a claim, for every expression. plan: Lean decides which certified imports (slide “Import rounds”) the slice may use. search: Lean looks for a proof of the claims.

Empty store

A cache keyed by a stage's exact inputs replays a stage it already holds. Every measurement here starts with it empty, so every stage runs. Times are of one check on a 12-core laptop, at commit ee0ed0e5 of the repository unless a slide says otherwise.
