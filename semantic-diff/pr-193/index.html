Read postconditions from the normal exit; imply an elements promise at a smaller predicate

no-behaviour-change

Why

Before the effect stores its exits (plan commit 10), every decision must read a postcondition from the exit that states it, never from an old field. The deciders read four: a fresh result store (a call whose result is fresh, a code's fresh result and the request's run bound), a fresh result object (call-fresh, Object.assign onto a constructed target), the receiver's elements where the result is true (call-elements, the order of signatures the search tries) and the index facts at the exit (fact survival at calls, a module row's conditions and realization, a stand-in's ensured facts, an import's signature). Each is now a reader of the normal exit's postconditions by its formula, and the consequence a call draws from it is the formula's meaning at the call, read from the exit's postconditions held there (Effect.callPost_holds), with no elaboration. Coverage had become "the same postcondition only" when it moved to schemas, where before an elements promise at a smaller predicate covered one at a larger: a target's postcondition is now implied by the same formula or by the receiver's elements at a predicate at most the target's (ElemPred.le), which restores that consequence.

Conjoin two signatures as the conjunction of their schemas, meeting class bounds in the frame

no-behaviour-change

Why

Plan step 4's conjunction (Exits.meet) kept the first frame and the second as a postcondition .transition, so coverage, which reads a frame, lost the meet of two closed bounds that today's Effect.meet computes. The schema conjunction now meets the frames where the two state the same entries beside their class entries: the meet of their class bounds with those entries, proved exact (a change a frame allows is one its class bound or its other entries allow). With that, combining two signatures of one value no longer needs an effect for the meet: the arrow rule combine asks the conjunction of the two schemas to cover the target's (Effect.meetCovers) and is proved from both exits holding at one invocation (PromiseWith.conj), their conjunction (Exits.exitPost_meet) and coverage. Two signatures conjoin where their promise and preconditions are the same, which is all the invocation argument reads; the frames' shapes, elements promises and element writes no longer need to agree. Today's Effect.meet and its consequence theorem are deleted.
