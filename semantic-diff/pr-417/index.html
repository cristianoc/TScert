State fill's element tag and entail guarded quantified clauses from known element tags

verified

Why

attachEmptyExtras (issue #393) returns new Array(chunks.length).fill(null) and states forall i in chunks: chunks[i] === null || result[i] === null || chunks[i].length <= result[i].length. It was refused: "a return of 'attachEmptyExtras' does not establish the postcondition". The fill's contract stated only the result's length, no rule derived a guarded quantified clause from an element tag known over a prefix, and a guarded clause required its guard's read to yield a value even where JavaScript evaluates it to undefined (chunks[i] at a hole) and the continuation decides the clause. Combines #411's second commit with #405/#407.

Soundness

The new meaning implies JavaScript's truth of the clause at every state where the bound array is an array. Every theorem reading a guarded body clause (renaming, framing, heap extension, lowering through read links, object merges, composition at calls, element-read instantiation) is re-proved against it, and the verifier builds with no sorry, axiom or warning. The rule's proof uses the source's tag where its continuation is impossible, the source's read where its target is not its own element, and alias equality of the two reads. mode8Accepted_sound and its premise are unchanged.
