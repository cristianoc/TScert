no-behaviour-change

Why

A signature's effect bounds the heap by three fields: a closed footprint, and two flags excepting the receiver's store or its own locations. The stated-effects design reads every effect as one regional frame at the call's positions (0 the receiver, i the i-th actual), so stated regions, native rows and the receiver flags are one language. The three fields had no such reading.

= [path 0 [] store: any]else where e excepts its store
= []else
with {v, s} the closed footprint; the own-locations case is tested
first, so the receiver's store, one of its own locations, stays at s.

addedTheorem the translation

within(e, r, h, h') ⟺ Post(frame(e), callEnv(r, args), h, h')
for every receiver r, actuals args and heaps h, h'

State a signature's frame as Modifies<P, T>, replacing Within<F, T> in one change

no-behaviour-change

Why

A statement's frame is to name what a call may change through its arguments and module cells (§2 of docs/drafts/stated-effects.md, as converged at 13ea2db2). Within<F, T>'s F had exactly the two class fields, so it had no place for a location, and its value/store spelling collides with module bindings of those names. The design settles one map P from what may change to how much, and one spelling.

addedRefusal the statement reader
